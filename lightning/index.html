Lightning → Thunder Lab

Acoustic propagation simulator

Sketch a lightning channel, position an observer on the ground, and listen to the resulting thunder. Sound from each channel segment travels to the observer at the speed of sound, so the shape and size of your lightning determine the timing and character of the rumble. During playback, blue wavefronts show direct sound spreading from the lightning, green wavefronts show sound reflected from the ground, and a white expanding circle marks which parts of the lightning you are hearing at that moment. Those intersections glow nearly white with a hint of pink. The white circle represents direct arrivals, not reflected echoes.

Ground acoustics

Rigid, flat-ground approximation using mirrored sound sources. The observer’s microphone is 1.7 m above the ground. Neon-green fronts show reflected sound; underground propagation is excluded.

Model: all channel segments discharge simultaneously. Each segment emits a low-frequency pressure pulse and contributes to a continuous, band-limited acoustic texture, delayed by its distance to the observer divided by the speed of sound. Segment strength scales with length and distance. Optional flat-ground image-source reflection; no ground transmission, wind, terrain scattering, or detailed nonlinear shock physics.

Playback begins at the lightning discharge (t = 0), including the full flash-to-thunder delay before the first acoustic arrival.
