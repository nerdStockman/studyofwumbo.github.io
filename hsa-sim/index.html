HSA Sim

The future value of paying a medical bill three different ways

A Health Savings Account can help twice: contributions avoid income tax, and money left invested in the HSA can grow tax-free. This simulator follows one paycheck and one medical expense forward through time. The no-HSA case is the baseline; the two HSA strategies are compared by how many extra dollars they produce above that baseline.

What the model assumes

The example uses a fixed $2,333.33 gross paycheck, $1,000 qualified medical expense, 25% paycheck tax rate, and 15% tax on brokerage gains. HSA contributions are made pre-tax. Brokerage principal is not taxed again at withdrawal, but its gains are taxed at 15%. HSA growth is untaxed, and HSA Receipt Hoarding assumes the final HSA withdrawal is fully covered by saved qualified medical receipts. The paycheck is intentionally set so that, after the HSA contribution, tax, and medical bill, HSA Receipt Hoarding has no cash left to invest in brokerage. Investment fees, inflation, contribution limits, state-specific HSA rules, and tax-bracket changes are not modeled.
