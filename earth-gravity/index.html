How it's calculated

WGS84 normal gravity at geodetic latitude φ is given by Somigliana's formula:

γ(φ) = γₑ × (1 + k sin²φ) / √(1 − e² sin²φ)

Using γₑ = 9.7803253359 m/s², k = 0.00193185265241, and e² = 0.00669437999013. The physical force is W = mγ newtons. The displayed apparent weight is the gram-equivalent reading on a scale calibrated at the North Pole: W / γ(90°). The plotted difference is Δm = m[γ(φ)/γ(90°) − 1] (in grams). The gravity value already includes the centrifugal effect of Earth's rotation.

At a given latitude, the WGS84 reference ellipsoid predicts the same normal gravity at all longitudes. Real gravity differs due to elevation, topography, and uneven mass distribution. The graph is a theoretical sea-level comparison, not a prediction of every physical scale reading.

Reference: National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency — WGS84.
